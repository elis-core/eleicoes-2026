Historical analysis of election results

The project compares rounds and elections by municipality and polling place to identify statistical deviations, examine their distribution and test for recurring patterns in other Brazilian states.

01Overview

This project compares presidential results across rounds and elections to identify deviations from historical and contextual models, quantify uncertainty and investigate whether geographical patterns recur. Version 0.3 retains the assessment of the two reviews and incorporates the subsequent counter-analysis, strengthening four operating rules.

5

Both rounds of the 2010, 2014, 2018, 2022 and 2026 elections.

92

The municipal pilot covers Rio de Janeiro municipalities where data coverage passes validation.

2

The first phase covers municipalities. The second expands to polling places and comparisons across states.

Original objective

Identify results that are statistically atypical under explicit, validated models, measure their magnitude and uncertainty, check for concentration within municipalities and test whether the results recur in comparable municipalities within and across states.

Araruama, Resende and Volta Redonda motivated the initial exploration. Their results have already been examined, so these municipalities cannot serve as blind cases or independent confirmatory evidence. Any classification as atypical still requires completed and validated analysis.

Status of version 0.4

The first empirical run reconciled official data from all five elections, checked the three 2026 examples and evaluated a historical baseline on 2022 data. Confirmatory analysis of 2026 requires further calibration.

The matrix of 21 decisions records how the project assessed the reviews. The counter-analysis section documents the four points adopted and the changes made in v0.3. The sidebar contents and earlier numerical examples remain available.

02Research questions

  1. How did the vote count for each finalist change between the first and second rounds of each election?
  2. How do these gains or losses compare with the votes cast for candidates eliminated in the first round?
  3. How much of the change in valid votes can be accounted for by changes in the electorate, abstention, blank votes and invalid votes?
  4. Are these changes consistent with the unit's history and with patterns in municipalities with similar characteristics?
  5. Are the 2026 deviations widespread or concentrated in particular polling places?
  6. Do municipalities with shared characteristics show similar patterns, first within the same state and then across states?

The analysis will cover both finalists in each election. It may examine deviations in different directions, including gains, losses, changes in participation and combinations of indicators.

Phase I · Municipal analysis. Ingest and reconcile official data, fit a parsimonious model, validate it on historical data and assess all eligible municipalities in Rio de Janeiro. The spatial structure will use municipalities. Nationwide matching of polling places is no longer a prerequisite for the pilot.

Phase II · Polling places and comparisons across states. Run descriptive screening and, where comparable historical data exist, assess polling places in every eligible pilot municipality, regardless of whether the municipality was flagged. Reserve in-depth documentary work and additional modelling for selected priorities and controls. Use other states for external validation, keeping exploratory adaptations separate from genuinely held-out tests.

Municipal flags will inform priorities for in-depth investigation. All eligible polling places will still enter screening. Deviations within municipalities can offset one another or be diluted in the municipal total. Fix the number and selection of controls for in-depth investigation before examining their polling-place results.

Retain polling sections as the source unit. Changes within a polling place or municipality can preserve its aggregate total. Transfers across units require reconciliation. Nearby buildings may serve different voters, and polling-place boundaries need not align with neighbourhoods.

Changes in scale affect results and correlations. Compare municipal and polling-place findings, treating records derived from the same voting machines as dependent. [R2]

Polling-place coverage independent of municipal results

  • Cross-sectional screening. Include all pilot polling places with reconciled data for the period under analysis, even where the historical match is insufficient. Describe rates and changes between comparable rounds. Record gaps in historical data separately from results within expected ranges.
  • Historical comparison. Include all polling places whose matches and data coverage meet predefined quality criteria. Eligibility is based on data quality and historical matching. It is independent of which candidate gained, the size of the gain ratio and municipal statistical significance.
  • In-depth investigation. Prioritise flagged patterns and controls for technical and documentary analysis. Record the basis for targeted selection.
  • Resource constraints. If full polling-place coverage is infeasible, report partial coverage. A sampling alternative requires a probability design, potentially stratified, with known inclusion probabilities and stated limits on generalisation. Disclose any departure from planned coverage, and distinguish screening from a list of extreme cases.

This work may follow the municipal pilot to fit operational capacity. The requirement governs how polling places are selected. The municipal panel can begin before full harmonisation of polling-place records.

04Data, governance and electoral register reconciliation

Required empirical source. Use primary files from the TSE Open Data Portal for results, vote count details, electorate, polling places, aggregates and transfers. Verify package and field names against each year's data dictionary, allowing for schema changes. Supplement municipal profiles with IBGE data.

Retain the secondary sources from v0.1 as a record of exploratory work and a point of comparison. The models will use validated primary data. Protocol preparation can begin before ingestion is complete. Freezing the confirmatory protocol requires the data quality report and an inventory of results already examined.

Traceability and reproducible execution

  • Preserve original files, URLs, filenames, licences, retrieval and generation dates, versions and SHA-256 hashes.
  • Keep original files, processed data, indicators, model fitting, validation and reporting in separate layers.
  • Record data dictionaries, reconciliation checks, exclusions, missing data and classification changes.
  • Version scripts, the environment, dependencies and random seeds. Provide a reproducible container configuration and instructions for running it.
  • Make materials available as permitted by their licences and provide a manifest sufficient for reproduction. Exclude unnecessary personal data from publication.

The hash verifies the identity of the preserved bytes, and the container supports a reproducible environment. Both support auditing the study. Establishing whether electronic records match each voter's intent requires further evidence.

Electoral populations and special categories

For each round, document the reference electorate, the people eligible to vote in the unit, actual turnout and the vote categories. Distinguish voters' original registration, temporary eligibility and polling destination as applicable to the office.

  • Voting away from the registered location and temporary transfers. Check origins and destinations to avoid double counting. The data must reflect the population eligible for the particular office and round. [D9]
  • Special polling sections. Identify and retain each category and its population. Justify and test any aggregation.
  • Notifications of absence. Treat these as records of absence. Exclude voters who notified an absence from turnout and vote counts. [D10]
  • Difference E₂ − E₁. Investigate differences using official data versions and definitions. Voting away from the registered location, transfers and adjustments are testable hypotheses. The causes of the differences in the three examples remain unverified.

Historical matches and confidence levels

Combine codes, names, addresses, coordinates, section composition and change records. Treat coordinates and IBGE boundary data as supporting evidence because a nearby new building may serve a different group of voters. Allow a match to remain unresolved if proximity is the only evidence.

Classify matches as confirmed, probable or unresolved using documented criteria. Review ambiguous cases manually, repeat the analyses using the most reliable subset and describe the excluded units. Distinguish polling-place addresses from voters' home addresses.

Proximity alone cannot promote a match from B or C to A. Retain the evidence, the rule applied, any necessary manual decision and the date of each match. For a documented split or merger, consider a stable aggregation across the historical series and specify how the unit of analysis changes.

Report the number of polling places and electorate covered in each class by municipality and election, together with reasons for exclusion. Compare the profiles of matched and unmatched units. Restrict conclusions about the absence of anomalies to the units assessed.

Finalists' gains relative to other candidates' votes

ΔFi = Fi,2 − Fi,1O1 = V1 − F1,1 − F2,1Ri = 100 × ΔFi / O1F denotes votes for a finalist, with subscripts 1 and 2 marking the first and second rounds. V denotes valid votes, O denotes votes for other candidates and Δ denotes the second round minus the first.

This measure is called gain relative to the first-round total for other candidates. It can be negative or exceed 100%. It relates aggregate changes. The data do not identify individual transfers. If O1 is zero, the ratio is undefined. Flag instability when the denominator is very small and display the counts.

Use the same definitions in every election. Comparisons by party or political bloc require a separate rule defined in advance, since candidates, alliances and opponents change across elections.

06Accounting reconciliation

The accounting identities help check the consistency of the data and decompose changes before statistical modelling.

Electorate, participation and valid votes

C = E − AV = E − A − B − NΔV = ΔE − ΔA − ΔB − ΔNE denotes the electorate, A abstention, C turnout, B blank votes, N invalid votes and V valid votes. These identities assume that all required categories are included and that the reference populations are reconciled.

If a separate, mutually exclusive category X is needed to reconcile turnout, the identity is V = E − A − B − N − X. Check the fields against the dictionary for each file and subtract each category only once.

Combined gains of the finalists

V1 = F1,1 + F2,1 + O1V2 = F1,2 + F2,2ΔF1 + ΔF2 = O1 + ΔV= O1 + ΔE − ΔA − ΔB − ΔN

A fall in abstention, blank votes or invalid votes adds to the net change in valid votes. A rise reduces it. This decomposition describes aggregate counts. The source of each candidate's gains remains unknown at voter level.

The identity also implies that R1 + R2 = 100 × (1 + ΔV/O1). The sum of the ratios can therefore exceed 100% when valid votes increase.

Use in the models

Reconciliation checks internal consistency. An independent test of electoral integrity requires separate evidence. Several indicators are mathematically related, so account for their dependence and avoid combinations that produce exact collinearity.

07Percentage comparisons

The project uses three complementary measures. State each column's denominator so that municipalities of different sizes can be compared.

Change relative to the indicator itself

gX = 100 × (X2 − X1) / X1Shows how much the count rose or fell relative to the first round. If X1 = 0, the percentage change is undefined. Report the absolute difference.

Contribution on a common denominator

100 × ΔV/E1 = 100 × ΔE/E1 − 100 × ΔA/E1 − 100 × ΔB/E1 − 100 × ΔN/E1All components use the first-round electorate as their denominator. Apply it to both the finalists' gains and votes for other candidates to preserve the accounting identity.

This normalisation allows contributions to be compared across municipalities. Express them as a percentage of the first-round electorate or as votes per thousand electors.

Change in rates in percentage points

The analysis also compares rates between rounds. The change in the abstention rate in percentage points is 100 × (A2/E2 − A1/E1). Candidate shares use V as their denominator. Rates for valid, blank and invalid votes use C. Abstention and turnout rates use E.

Relative change in a count, a change in rates in percentage points and a contribution relative to E1 answer different questions. A large percentage reduction in blank votes, for example, may represent only a small share of the electorate.

09Core, spatial and transition models

The analysis starts with a parsimonious model and simple benchmarks. Additional complexity is warranted if it improves calibration, out-of-sample prediction and the ability to detect specified simulated perturbations. A percentage for discriminatory power has yet to be established.

Core compositional model for counts

The mutually exclusive categories in one round form a composition when divided by the same E. In the first round, the categories are the two finalists, other candidates, blank votes, invalid votes, abstention and any additional categories required. In the second round, the other-candidates category is structurally absent and is excluded from the active categories. Rates with different denominators and signed gains between rounds are analysed separately from the components of a single composition.

Ym,t = (F₁, F₂, O, B, N, A, X)m,t, with the active categories summing to Em,tThe leading candidate model is a hierarchical logistic-normal multinomial for Y conditional on E.Sensitivity models include the Dirichlet-multinomial and beta-binomial models for separately defined binary outcomes.The index m denotes a municipality or polling place, t denotes an election or round, and X contains only the other categories required. A single beta-binomial model covers only one binary outcome. Changes in E will also be reconciled and examined in a separate module.

The models will condition second-round outcomes on first-round indicators, historical data and a small set of contextual covariates. Regularising priors, comparison with simple benchmarks and external validation will limit overfitting. The election counts cover the full population. The relevant uncertainty concerns the behavioural model and its assumptions.

ALR and ILR may be used in sensitivity analyses for positive proportions. CLR retains a sum constraint and leaves some singularity. Log-ratios cannot be applied directly to negative gains or zeros. Any pseudocounts must be disclosed. Count models must distinguish observed zeros from structural zeros. [R4]

Stabilisation while retaining the counts

Adopt hierarchical effects with partial pooling for rate and probability parameters, so estimates for small units can draw on comparable groups. Always report counts, raw rates, regularised estimates and intervals. The ratio ΔF/O₁ remains descriptive. Its uncertainty will come from the joint model, with the ratio itself left unsmoothed.

Shrinkage can reduce extremes caused by variability and can also attenuate genuine departures. Compare results with and without shrinkage and assess sensitivity to priors. The ratio remains undefined when O₁ is zero.

Explicit spatial dependence

Construct W from the IBGE municipal boundaries, defining neighbours as municipalities that share a boundary. Document the version, symmetry, connections and units with no neighbours. Prespecify k-nearest neighbours as an alternative and justify the treatment of disconnected units.

Compare the hierarchical model without a spatial structure with a BYM2 extension combining structured and unstructured spatial effects. Document the scale and priors. This structure models dependence. Distinguishing legitimate political change from manipulation requires additional evidence. Assess how much smoothing absorbs collective patterns and whether prediction improves in held-out spatial blocks. [R5]

Ecological inference as a supplementary module

The project will examine transition matrices using hierarchical R × C models where comparable units and sufficient information exist. Choose the R × C extension explicitly. Check whether a 2 × 2 implementation addresses the same modelling question before substituting it. [R6]

The matrices show model estimates of individual flows from aggregate data. Different matrices can reproduce the same margins. Report feasible accounting bounds, intervals and sensitivity to priors, covariates and assumptions, including cases where the information cannot support a precise estimate. This identification problem is fundamental to ecological inference. [R7]

Reconcile the electorates across rounds before fitting transitions. Even E₁ = E₂ can mask changes in the individual electors. Entries, exits and transfers require compatible categories or sensitivity analyses. With aggregate margins alone, the matrix may remain only partially identified. The module will be implemented in stages. The validity of the wider aggregate analysis will be assessed separately.

Four previous elections still provide a short time series. More municipalities do not create more independent state or national shocks. Historical data provide a benchmark. An undetectable mechanism may produce the same pattern as the benchmark, and the comparison cannot certify previous elections.

Distinguishing observations, accounting identities and estimates

Ecological inference remains an optional follow-up module. Aggregate deviation analysis can proceed when the data and calibration are adequate, even if that module is omitted, fails to converge or lacks sufficient identification.

  • Observed. Counts and records present in the sources, subject to documentary validation.
  • Derived from accounting identities. Net changes, rates and breakdowns that follow from the defined identities.
  • Model estimates. Predictions, transition matrices and latent quantities, accompanied by assumptions and uncertainty.

Transition tables and charts must identify the third group explicitly. Avoid “actual flow”, “observed transfer” and “recovered true matrix” for cells inferred from aggregate margins alone. A Sankey diagram based on estimates must be clearly labelled and supplemented with a display of uncertainty. Use accounting waterfall charts where reliable estimates are unavailable.

10Comparable municipalities and external validation

Separate the characteristics used to assess comparability from the indicators that define an anomaly. Describe the initial municipality by size, electoral history, voter registration profile, socioeconomic context, geography, election administration and data quality.

Small initial set of covariates

Prioritise the log of the electorate, first-round composition, harmonised turnout and vote histories, age and education, and comparable measures of urbanisation and socioeconomic conditions. Add the operational inventory described in section 09. Reserve manually coded qualitative covariates, such as local endorsements, for specific hypotheses and justified extensions.

Use sources with compatible reference periods. Information must have been available before the election being predicted. For example, a prospective validation for 2014 cannot use 2022 data. Label retrospective contextual analysis separately.

Auditable similarity rule

  1. Define covariates, transformations and the treatment of missing values using the development data.
  2. Use Mahalanobis distance with regularised covariance and common support, or coarsened exact matching (CEM) stratification as an alternative analysis. Remove redundant dimensions.
  3. Set maximum distances (calipers) and comparison groups before examining held-out results. Report units without a suitable match.
  4. Check covariate balance and document the remaining differences.
  5. Compare flagged and unflagged cases within the same state. Then test the rule in comparable held-out states and document any revisions to the model.

A “multidimensional propensity score” will not be used to predict the anomaly classification itself and select controls on that basis. A conventional propensity score assumes a defined exposure. The initial aim here is descriptive similarity, which does not by itself identify a causal effect. Distance measures and calipers can be applied independently of a propensity score. [R8]

Pattern to replicate

Record the direction and size of deviations, each finalist's gain relative to O₁, gains relative to E₁, changes in turnout and invalid votes, predictive uncertainty, and concentration across polling places. The ΔF/O₁ ratios are dependent on the underlying counts and will be treated accordingly.

A reduction in abstention, holding the other components constant, increases the finalists' combined gains under the accounting identity. The identity cannot tell which candidate gains votes. A candidate's ratio above 100% is insufficient to classify the result as an anomaly.

11Protocol, false discoveries and robustness

Prospective protocol freeze and disclosure of prior analysis

Record the questions, rules, exclusions, primary indicators, candidate models, decision criteria, seeds and validation plan publicly or in a dated repository. List all results already examined and distinguish exploratory work from confirmation. Tests of the three cases already examined must be described as unblinded.

Claims of blinded assessment require genuinely held-out data and documented outcome access. Public availability does not establish blinding. Any change after examining a validation sample makes that round part of development and requires a new independent test.

Test families and false discovery control

  • Proposed primary family. One two-sided joint test per eligible Rio de Janeiro municipality in 2026, with 92 hypotheses expected. The joint statistic must respect accounting dependencies. Reporting its components provides descriptive detail.
  • Calibration. Produce valid p-values through out-of-sample prediction or simulation under the null model. Refit the pipeline where necessary and preserve dependence. Verify calibration before treating predictive tail probabilities or in-sample fitted residuals as uniform p-values.
  • Default rule. Use a nominal FDR of 5% with Benjamini–Yekutieli (BY) and valid p-values to account for unknown dependence. Use Benjamini–Hochberg (BH) for sensitivity analysis, or as the primary rule only if its dependence assumptions are justified before the held-out test. [R9]
  • Diagnostics and expansion. Register separate families for components, polling places and states. Selecting polling places after flagging requires selective or hierarchical adjustment, or a clear exploratory designation. The correction must account for how each subset was selected.
  • Magnitude. Report counts, percentage points, effects per thousand electors and intervals. Set a substantive prioritisation threshold based on the study objectives and simulations before examining confirmatory results.

A 5% FDR limits the expected proportion of false rejections under the procedure's assumptions. It gives neither the probability of fraud in a municipality nor the exact error proportion in a particular list. Local FDR is an exploratory option given the 92 municipalities. Narrower confirmatory claims may require family-wise error control, such as Holm's procedure.

Validation and criteria for proceeding

  • Temporal. Test a prediction for 2022 fitted on 2010–2018 data using temporally compatible variables. Use other forecast origins where feasible.
  • Spatial. Validate on held-out spatial blocks and then in other states, assessing covariate support and institutional differences.
  • Calibration and power. Measure interval coverage and false positives. Assess sensitivity in known synthetic scenarios. Sensitivity to actual fraud remains unverified.
  • Model. Compare simple benchmarks, the hierarchical core model and the spatial extension. Check sensitivity to priors, overdispersion and shrinkage.
  • Quality. Repeat using more reliable historical links and documented alternative rules for missing values, away-from-home voting and special polling sections.
  • Scale. Compare municipality and polling-place results while avoiding pseudoreplication. Include unflagged controls to assess information lost through aggregation.
  • Governance. Record the chosen configuration before opening each external test set and keep a version history of all changes.
When tests can be interpreted

If historical and simulation-based calibration is inadequate, the pilot will report descriptive results and diagnose limitations. Confirmatory identification of statistically unusual municipalities requires adequate calibration. BY and BH both require valid p-values and cannot fix model misspecification.

Empirical assessment of parsimony

Treat the lean model as an operational preference pending measurement. Define benchmarks and measure gains or losses from adding or removing groups of covariates.

  1. Compare the simple historical benchmark, lean core model and extensions on the same held-out temporal and spatial data.
  2. Assess calibration, interval coverage, predictive quality and stability of flagged cases. Quantify uncertainty in the comparisons.
  3. Measure sensitivity and false positives in explicitly defined synthetic scenarios. Report the scope of these scenarios separately from unknown real-world irregularities.
  4. Record data acquisition costs, computing time and harmonisation effort alongside the measured benefits.
  5. Preregister variable retention rules. Retain necessary controls unless the evidence supports removing them. An unverified claim of “90% of the power” is insufficient.

Report local coverage separately for municipalities flagged or unflagged at the aggregate level, eligible polling places in each group, places examined and places excluded on quality grounds. This table will show whether the analysis has inadvertently reintroduced the rejected municipal filter.

Reconciled countsThe fields and totals are consistent with the verified identities.
Gain ratio above 100%One candidate gains more votes than the other candidates received in the first round. The ratio alone does not measure improbability.
Statistical deviationThe observed result is unusual under the model and its assumptions, with quantified uncertainty.
Recurrence in comparable municipalitiesThe pattern may recur. Test this externally and compare with cases within the expected range.
Operational or territorial associationThe relationship warrants investigation and may be affected by confounding.
Attribution to a software alterationRequires evidence about the mechanism in addition to aggregate distributions.

Technically supportable conclusion. “These units have statistically unusual results under the historical and contextual models tested, with the magnitude and uncertainty reported. The territorial analysis shows whether deviations are widespread or concentrated. External tests assess whether they recur in comparable municipalities.”

Improbability depends on the model. A deviation may reflect legitimate changes the model missed, poor model fit, data issues, administrative errors or irregularities. A hypothesis of software alteration requires additional evidence about the mechanism.

Aggregate results can reveal patterns and guide investigation. An alteration could produce a distribution indistinguishable from legitimate voting. Certification of process integrity requires more than the absence of an anomaly.

15Counter-analysis and design refinements

Document assessed: Contra análise dos pareceres do Proj v.01 A.Eleitoral, a one-page edition credited to Cláudio Stegun on 3 November 2026. The text compares the two reviews with v0.1 and identifies four extrapolations. [P3]

Assessment

The project accepts all four points. They are consistent with the aim of detecting aggregate deviations within the limits of what those data reveal about individual behaviour. Version 0.2 incorporated all four qualifications. Version 0.3 makes them explicit in the criteria for selection, linkage, communication and validation.

PointDecisionPrevious approach and refinement
CA01 · Municipal filterAgreedVersion 0.2 allowed municipalities without flags, but its description of Phase II still focused on flagged cases and a sample of controls. Version 0.3 distinguishes screening of all eligible polling places, historical comparison with assessed links and targeted investigation.
CA02 · Actual flowsAgreedVersion 0.2 already acknowledged that the data cannot identify individual flows and treated R × C as supplementary. Version 0.3 labels observations, derivations and estimates and allows aggregate analysis to proceed without a transition matrix.
CA03 · Geography as a guaranteeAgreedVersion 0.2 already treated coordinates as supporting evidence. Version 0.3 puts classes A/B/C into practice, separates primary use from sensitivity analysis and requires a report on coverage and exclusions.
CA04 · More than 90% of powerAgreedVersion 0.2 already rejected this claim. Version 0.3 requires comparison of specifications on the same holdout sets, using calibration, prediction, simulated sensitivity and cost metrics.

Scope of the critiques

The reviews proposed several approaches the project retains. Ecological models can produce conditional estimates, coordinates can support harmonisation, simpler models can reduce costs, and flagged municipalities can receive priority for investigation. Selection remains open to other municipalities. Individual flows, continuity of the electorate and quantitative performance each require supporting evidence.

The project retains its phased implementation and the pilot covering Rio de Janeiro's 92 municipalities. The counter-analysis evaluates the methodology. The revised design retains empirical evaluation and data checks as separate stages.

17First empirical run and pending proposal

Reconciled municipal panel

The pilot integrated ten TSE files for 2010, 2014, 2018, 2022 and 2026 and recorded them in a SHA-256 manifest. The panel contains 920 municipality–round records, representing 460 pairs across rounds. The detailed count and votes by candidate matched across 2,148 electoral-zone keys. All accounting residuals checked were zero, including in the reconciliation of finalists' gains.

For 2010 and 2014, the pilot used votes by candidate because there was no separate field for valid votes by candidate. Their sum was checked against the official total for each electoral-zone key. The panel retains the published population of eligible voters and separates records marked as votes cast away from home. In 2026, the TSE reported 39 duplicate origin links nationwide. Any local adjustment requires documentary evidence of how those links are distributed across municipalities. TSE, 2026 Results.

The benchmark predicts the change between rounds per 100 first-round eligible voters using the municipal median for 2010, 2014 and 2018. For finalists' gains, it aligns their positions by their first-round vote totals in each municipality.
